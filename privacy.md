# Privacy policy

**Inkfold Notebooks** for iPhone and iPad<br>
Effective November 1, 2026

<p class="lead">The short version: Inkfold collects nothing about you. There's no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and there are no Inkfold servers. The app makes no network calls except to Apple's App Store, for the Unlimited Notebooks purchase, to download a public-domain book you asked for, and to the book catalogs you added yourself (which receive the username and password you gave for that server). Your notebooks stay on your device.</p>

## What stays on your device

- **Your notebooks:** their covers, pages, ink and typed text, kept in the app's own storage on your iPhone or iPad.
- **Your books:** the copies of the EPUB, PDF and text files you add, and your place in each, kept the same way. Inkfold opens them itself and never sends them anywhere.
- **Your settings:** finger drawing, the shelf's tilt and wood, the pens you last chose, and whether Unlimited Notebooks was bought on this device's Apple Account.
- **Handwriting, read for search:** Inkfold reads your handwriting as text with Apple's text recognition, which runs on the device. The text is kept with the page it came from, so search can find it, and goes nowhere else.

Your device's own backups (iCloud Backup, or a backup to your computer) include the app's notebooks, as they do for any app. Those backups are Apple's, under your settings.

## The purchase

Unlimited Notebooks is bought through Apple's App Store, with Apple's own purchase sheet. Apple handles the payment; Inkfold never sees your payment details, your Apple Account or your name. The app only asks the App Store whether this Apple Account bought it, and its price in your currency. Apple's privacy policy covers the App Store.

## Public-domain books

Twelve public-domain books come with the app: putting one on the shelf needs no connection. The others on the list (the shelf's + menu, *Add a Public-Domain Book…*, under *More to Download*) are downloaded from [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org) only when you tap one. Inkfold then asks Project Gutenberg's website for that book's file, and that request is all it sends: it names the app and its support page, and carries no account, identifier, cookie or anything else about you. Like any website, Project Gutenberg sees the request come from your device's internet (IP) address; its [privacy policy](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/privacy_policy.html) says it deletes those logs after at most 60 days. Inkfold keeps and collects nothing from the download. The book is then a copy on your device, like any book you add.

## Your own book catalogs

You can add a book catalog of your own (the shelf's + menu, *Add from a Catalog…*): an OPDS server you run or use, such as Calibre-Web on a computer at home. Inkfold connects to it only while you browse it or download a book from it, and only to the address you gave. It sends that server your requests for its pages and books, and the username and password you gave for it, when the server asks for them; nothing else, and nothing to anyone else. The password is kept in your device's Keychain, never in a file, and the name, address and username with the app's settings, on your device. On your home network a catalog may use plain http, which sends the password unencrypted over that network; anywhere else Inkfold uses only https, and it won't connect to a server whose certificate your device doesn't trust. The first time it reaches your home network, iOS asks you to allow Local Network access; you can change that in Settings > Privacy & Security > Local Network. What the server keeps is up to whoever runs it.

## What leaves your device

Only what you send yourself, the request for a public-domain book you asked for, and what goes to the catalogs you added yourself (above). When you share a page or a notebook as a PDF, the share sheet sends it where you choose: Mail, Messages, AirDrop, the Files app or a printer. Inkfold doesn't send anything else by itself.

## Permissions

Inkfold doesn't ask for your camera, microphone, photos, contacts or location; it asks for Local Network access only when you open a book catalog at a home address. The *Support* and *Privacy Policy* links in the shelf's ••• menu open these pages in Safari. The bookshelf reads the device's tilt (its motion sensors) to lean the books, which needs no permission and never leaves the app. You can turn it off in the shelf's ••• menu.

## Children

Inkfold isn't directed at children, and it doesn't collect anything from anyone.

## Crash reports

If you've chosen in iOS to share analytics with app developers, Apple may pass on crash reports, which hold no notebook content. That's Apple's setting, and it's yours to change.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date.
