# Privacy policy

**Inkfold Notebooks** for iPhone and iPad<br>
Effective November 13, 2026

<p class="lead">The short version: Inkfold collects nothing about you. There's no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and there are no Inkfold servers. The app makes no network calls except to Apple's App Store, for the Unlimited Notebooks purchase; to Project Gutenberg, to download a public-domain book you take home and for the cover pictures of the public-domain books standing on the Bookshop's shelves (nothing about you is sent); and to the book catalogs you added yourself (which receive the username and password you gave for that server). Your notebooks stay on your device, and in a folder of your own iCloud Drive if you turn on its sync.</p>

## What stays on your device

- **Your notebooks:** their covers, pages, ink and typed text, kept in the app's own storage on your iPhone or iPad.
- **Your books:** the copies of the EPUB, PDF, text and comic (CBZ) files you add, and your place in each, kept the same way. Inkfold opens them itself and never sends them anywhere.
- **Your settings:** finger drawing, the shelf's tilt and wood, the pens you last chose, and whether Unlimited Notebooks was bought on this device's Apple Account.
- **Handwriting, read for search:** Inkfold reads your handwriting as text with Apple's text recognition, which runs on the device. The text is kept with the page it came from, so search can find it, and goes nowhere else. Converting handwriting to typed text uses the same recognition, on the device.
- **Photos and voice memos on a page:** a photo you add is a copy of the one you picked, and a voice memo is what you recorded; both are kept inside the notebook, like its ink.
- **Locked diaries:** a notebook you lock opens after Face ID (or your passcode). iOS checks your face; Inkfold only learns whether it was you, and never sees or keeps anything about your face.

Your device's own backups (iCloud Backup, or a backup to your computer) include the app's notebooks, as they do for any app. Those backups are Apple's, under your settings.

## The diagnostics log

Inkfold keeps a log of what it does, to help find bugs: where a tap landed, which page took the keyboard, where the caret went, when it synced, and if Inkfold crashes, where in its own code (the system's report of the crash too, which iOS hands the app at its next launch). It never holds what you type or draw (a key is logged only as "a letter", "a space" or "a return"), and it stays on your device, kept to its last megabyte. If you sync with iCloud Drive, a copy goes into the sync folder's Diagnostics folder, in your own iCloud Drive; *Share Diagnostics Log…* sends it where you choose. In Inkfold, ••• > Settings > Keep a Diagnostics Log turns it off.

## iCloud Drive sync (if you turn it on)

You can keep one library on your iPhone and your iPad (the shelf's ••• menu, *Sync with iCloud Drive…*): you pick a folder in your own iCloud Drive, and Inkfold copies your notebooks, your books, what you drew in them and the list of your books into it, and brings in what your other devices put there. iCloud Drive moves the folder between your devices under your Apple Account, as it does for any files you keep there; Inkfold itself makes no network connection for it and has no server. The folder is yours: you can see it in the Files app, and *Stop Syncing* leaves it as it is. Apple's privacy policy covers iCloud.

## The purchase

Unlimited Notebooks is bought through Apple's App Store, with Apple's own purchase sheet. Apple handles the payment; Inkfold never sees your payment details, your Apple Account or your name. The app only asks the App Store whether this Apple Account bought it, and its price in your currency. Apple's privacy policy covers the App Store.

## Public-domain books

Twelve public-domain books and *Poses*, a book of public-domain photographs for drawing, come with the app: putting one on the shelf needs no connection. The others on the list (the *Bookshop*'s own shelves, when you take one home; or its shelf *Your Own Books* > *Public Domain*, the rest of *Art Reference* and *More to Download*) are downloaded from [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org) only when you tap one, or take it home from the Bookshop. Inkfold then asks Project Gutenberg's website for that book's file, and that request is all it sends: it names the app and its support page, and carries no account, identifier, cookie or anything else about you. Like any website, Project Gutenberg sees the request come from your device's internet (IP) address; its [privacy policy](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/privacy_policy.html) says it deletes those logs after at most 60 days. Inkfold keeps and collects nothing from the download. The book is then a copy on your device, like any book you add. The Bookshop also asks Project Gutenberg for the cover pictures of these books (below), in the same way.

## Your own book catalogs

You can add a book catalog of your own (the Bookshop's shelf *Your Own Books* > *Catalog*, or its *Set Up the Shop*): an OPDS server you run or use, such as Calibre-Web on a computer at home. Inkfold connects to it only while you browse it (in that list, or in the Bookshop, below) or download a book from it, and only to the address you gave. It sends that server your requests for its pages and books, and the username and password you gave for it, when the server asks for them; nothing else, and nothing to anyone else. The password is kept in your device's Keychain, never in a file, and the name, address and username with the app's settings, on your device. On your home network a catalog may use plain http, which sends the password unencrypted over that network; anywhere else Inkfold uses only https, and it won't connect to a server whose certificate your device doesn't trust. The first time it reaches your home network, iOS asks you to allow Local Network access; you can change that in Settings > Privacy & Security > Local Network. What the server keeps is up to whoever runs it.

## The Bookshop

The Bookshop (the shelf's + knob) is where books, blank journals and things for your shelves come from: they stand on its shelves to take home. Nothing in it is sold, and it has no account. Its shelves of public-domain books and their descriptions come from the list inside the app, and the framed pictures on its shelves are public-domain pictures from museums' open collections that come with the app: showing them asks nothing of anyone. When one of the books Inkfold lists from Project Gutenberg stands on a shelf you're looking at, Inkfold asks Project Gutenberg's website for that book's cover picture: one request for the picture, naming the app and its version (and its support page) as the download does, and nothing about you. The picture is kept on your device (with the app's own data, and left out of backups), so it isn't asked for again; until it comes, or if there is none, the book has a cover the app draws. The books that come with the app show their own covers and ask nothing. Taking a book home asks Project Gutenberg for that book's file, as above. Each time you open the Bookshop it asks each catalog you've switched on in *Set Up the Shop* for its list of shelves and for the books on them, for a part of the catalog (its authors, a series) when you choose that part, and for the cover pictures of the books you see, exactly as browsing that catalog does (above). What a catalog answered (its books' titles, authors, descriptions and links; never your password) is kept on your device, in the app's cache, which the system may clear, so the shop's shelves are there at once the next time you open it; a catalog you delete has it removed. A catalog's cover picture is asked only of the catalog's own server, with the username and password you gave for it, and is kept on your device (in the app's cache, which the system may clear) so it needn't be asked for again; no cover is asked of anyone but that catalog and Project Gutenberg; a catalog you switch off there is asked nothing. If you choose a folder of your own books for it, Inkfold remembers which folder (with the app's settings, on your device) and reads the names and sizes of the book files in it; a book you take home from it is copied, and the folder is never changed. Which bookcase a book goes to, the basket, and what the shop stocks stay on your device.

## What leaves your device

Only what you send yourself, the requests to Project Gutenberg for a public-domain book you take home and for the covers of the ones on the Bookshop's shelves, and what goes to the catalogs you added yourself (above). When you share a page or a notebook as a PDF, the share sheet sends it where you choose: Mail, Messages, AirDrop, the Files app or a printer. Inkfold doesn't send anything else by itself.

## Permissions

Inkfold doesn't ask for your camera, contacts or location. It asks for the microphone only when you first record a voice memo, and records only while you're recording one. It never asks for your photo library: when you add a photo to a page, iOS's own picker gives Inkfold just the photo you chose. Face ID is used only to open a notebook you locked. Siri and the Shortcuts app can open a notebook or add words to one when you ask them to; that happens on your device. Inkfold asks for Local Network access only when you open a book catalog at a home address. The *Support* and *Privacy Policy* links in the shelf's ••• menu open these pages in Safari. The bookshelf reads the device's tilt (its motion sensors) to lean the books, which needs no permission and never leaves the app. You can turn it off in the shelf's ••• menu.

## Children

Inkfold isn't directed at children, and it doesn't collect anything from anyone.

## Crash reports

If you've chosen in iOS to share analytics with app developers, Apple may pass on crash reports, which hold no notebook content. That's Apple's setting, and it's yours to change.

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date.
